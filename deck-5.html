One real tool, for a real person, live on the internet.

Brief builder

Intake answers in. A structured first-draft brief out.

2

Performance digest

Upload the weekly export. Read what changed.

3

Competitor watch

Watch public pages. Hear only when one changes.

4

Pitch board

Type an idea. Get taglines and concept images.

Notes: Better still is the task each student wrote down in Phase 1. The third idea reads other people’s sites: public pages only, and check each site’s terms.
Slide 4 of 8

What it must have

THE PRODUCT

It works, and it is safe

Live. Sign-in. Private data per user. A job that reports failure. No secrets in the repository.

THE HARNESS

Agents can work on it

A cited runbook with gates. A planner and reviewer that were used. A skill and a hook.

THE PROCESS

The student directed it

A scope, a plan with I can lines, one feature through the full pipeline, an undo written before each merge.

Notes: This is the checklist students work to and the one you can assess against.
Slide 5 of 8

Seven stages

StageWhat comes out
1. Define doneThree to five sentences starting with “I can…”
2. ScopeA short document: in, out, constraints, risks.
3. PlanSeparate pieces of work, each with its own acceptance lines.
4. Harness firstRunbook, agents and gates in place before any code.
5. Live on day oneThe smallest deployable thing, deployed.
6. BuildOne piece at a time, each through the pipeline.
7. HardenTwo-account test, a forced failure, a secrets scan, a stranger.
Notes: Stage 5 matters most. Deploying something nearly empty on day one surfaces every account and key problem while the app is too small for anything else to be wrong.
Slide 6 of 8

What students hand in

ItemWhat it shows
Live address and a test accountIt works.
Access to the repositoryHow it was built, including the runbook’s history.
Decision logThe calls the agents made, and that the student knew.
Two-minute demoEach I can line, done on screen.
One-page retrospectiveWhat went wrong, and which rule each mistake produced.
Notes: The retrospective is the part to weight. Anyone can end up with a working app. Explaining why each runbook rule exists shows the student was directing.
Slide 7 of 8

One way to assess it

QuestionEvidence
Does it work?Every I can line passes, done by someone other than the builder.
Is it safe?No cross-user data, no secrets in history, a clear list of gates.
Could someone else pick it up?A fresh agent session can state the rules and resume from the notes.
Did the student direct it?Scope was cut on purpose. Decisions are logged. Rules trace to mistakes.
Notes: Weights are yours to set. All four can be answered from what is handed in.
