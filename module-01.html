Why this matters

The job is changing from doing the work to directing the work. This module explains what that means for someone going into advertising, and what you'll be able to do by the end of this guide.

15 minutes Nothing to install

From doing to directing

Right now you probably use AI the way most people do. You ask a question, you get an answer, you copy it somewhere. That is useful, and it is also the smallest version of what these tools can do.

The larger version looks different. You describe an outcome. A group of AI agents plans the work, does it, checks it and reports back. You make the decisions that matter and approve the result. Your skill stops being "I can write this" and becomes "I can get this built, and I know whether it's right."

My bet is that many people entering advertising now will spend much of their careers working this way. They will run teams of agents, or build the systems that do the work, more than they do every task by hand.

Doing the work
You ask a chatbot for ten headline options and pick one.
You pull last week's numbers into a spreadsheet every Monday.
You check competitor ads when you remember to.
You rewrite the same brief template for every client.
Directing the work
You build a tool that drafts headlines against the brief and flags the ones that break brand rules.
A scheduled job pulls the numbers and sends you a summary before you're awake.
A monitor checks competitor ads daily and tells you only when something changes.
A small app turns a client intake form into a finished first-draft brief.

I started where you are

I'm Jack. I studied advertising at Iowa State and graduated in 2014. My career has been in sales and go-to-market work. I am not an engineer by training. I taught myself everything in this guide.

Two or three years ago I started using ChatGPT for fun. I had it write stories and answer questions. Eventually I asked it how I could scrape a webpage for data. That produced my first Python script, written completely by ChatGPT, along with instructions for installing the tools it needed, like Python and Homebrew, and for running it. It didn't work. So I revised, and revised, and revised, and revised, and revised.

Today I run a setup where a group of AI agents plans, builds, reviews and ships software for me, and much of it happens while I'm doing something else.

I'm telling you this for one reason. Nothing between those two points required a talent you don't have. It required patience, a willingness to be confused for a while, and using my brain when the agent got something wrong. If I got here, you can.

By the end of this guide you will be able to

Work in a terminal without fear
Open one, move around your files, and run Claude Code inside a project.
Put a real app on the internet
With sign-in, a database, and jobs that run on a schedule.
Build tools that live on your own computer
Scripts, desktop apps, and even a small game.
Write the runbook your agents follow
The file that tells them how you work, what done means, and what they must never do.
Run a team, not one assistant
One agent plans, another builds, another reviews, and you approve.
Tell good output from bad
Know what to check, when to push back, and when to start over.

What to expect

This is not a coding course
You will learn to read what an agent wrote and judge it. You will not be asked to write programs from scratch.
It is not automatic
Agents get things wrong with total confidence. Your judgment is the part that makes the output worth using.
Things will break
Error messages are normal. Every module lists the common ones and what to do. Pasting the error back to the agent solves most of them.
The first week is the hardest
Setup has the most unfamiliar words. Once your first small project is live, the rest is repetition with new tools.
